I'm Shivam Gupta, and this is PartCue: every part has a way home.

PartCue is a spatial memory of your own teardown. It is designed for repair benches where your hands are busy and your eyes should stay on the work.

This demonstration runs in Lens Studio's preview. The enclosure and tray are simulated, and every part is marked manually. PartCue is not recognizing parts through a camera.

First, I place the workspace. Then I select a part, choose an empty tray well, and record its removal. I do the same for the other parts. Each record connects a saved origin to a tray location, in the order I removed it.

Now I choose Return parts. The last removal becomes the first return. The amber tray cue tells me where to pick up the part. The cyan origin cue shows where I marked it came from. The path connects them directly in the workspace.

Watch what happens when I choose the wrong part. PartCue tells me which one is next, and progress stays unchanged. I select the expected part, put it back, and confirm. That confirmation is mine; it is not an automatic inspection.

If I need to step away, I pause. The cues disappear. Resume brings me back to the same next part. Undo restores the previous return when I need to correct myself.

At the end, every recorded part is accounted for and the active cues disappear. That means the recorded returns are confirmed, not that the device has passed a safety or function test.

I built PartCue with AI assistance around one question: can spatial memory make reassembly easier than reconstructing notes and photos? The next step is testing recording overhead with repair technicians. Twenty dollars per bench per month is a pricing hypothesis, not validated demand.

PartCue does not guess the repair. It helps you find your own way back.
